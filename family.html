What family judges often focus on

  • Best interests of the child
  • Safety, stability, and routine
  • Credibility and consistency
  • Evidence, not just accusation

What helps most

Specific examples, a clean chronology, and records connected to the actual issues in dispute.

Important things people often do not know

Family matters can involve different conference stages, and each stage has different purposes and documents. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Case Conference

Usually an early meaningful appearance

Ontario says a case conference is usually the first meaningful appearance before a judge after first appearance court. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Settlement Conference

Focused on narrowing or resolving issues

Ontario says settlement conferences include exploring settlement, narrowing issues, ensuring disclosure, and even obtaining a view of how the court might decide the case. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

Trial Management

Used to get the case ready for trial

Ontario says trial management conferences are intended to get everyone ready for trial and deal with evidence, witnesses, timing, and trial planning. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

Common family court forms and process points

Ontario’s Family Law Rules forms page includes conference and motion-related family forms, including Forms 14, 14A, 14B, 14C, and 17-series conference forms. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}

Common forms
  • Form 14 — Notice of Motion
  • Form 14A — Affidavit (General)
  • Form 14B — Motion Form
  • Form 17-series conference forms
What usually helps most
  • A clean chronology
  • Specific examples, not broad accusations
  • Messages, schedules, records, and proof tied to events
  • A child-focused explanation of what order is sought and why
